Whether this fits.

It fits if

  • Partners are at capacity while the pipeline is still growing.
  • The no-bid call keeps arriving after drafting has started.
  • Pricing logic and win themes get rebuilt from memory each cycle.
  • Delivery starts from recollection of what was promised.
  • A client has asked how the firm uses AI on their work.

It does not fit if

  • You want a tool selected and installed rather than a workflow understood.
  • You want AI to make the pursue-or-decline call. It should not, and here it will not.
  • You need a compliance certification. This is operational work, not an audit or an attestation.
  • Nobody senior can spare time in the two weeks. The mapping needs the people who do the work.

Why the controls are the point, not the paperwork.

Speed that a partner cannot stand behind is worthless in this business, so the assurance layer is designed in rather than added afterwards. Access is scoped to the pursuit team. Reuse comes only from an approved library with provenance attached. The partner review of promise, price, and team stays exactly where it is, with a named approver on the record.

Published research on professional work finds that AI raises quality on tasks inside its capability and degrades it outside, which is the evidenced reason review and task routing are permanent controls here rather than launch-phase precautions (Dell'Acqua et al., 2023; a field experiment at one elite firm, task-level results, not a claim about your firm's margin).

This is operational guidance. It is not legal, tax, accounting, audit, or regulatory-certification advice, and nothing here replaces professional judgment.
