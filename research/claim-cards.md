# Claim cards: the publication-language authority

**Status:** Current publication authority. Written 2026-08-02 against the WS-19
validation pass.
**Binds:** every public asset in this repository. Playbooks, outcome pages,
persona pages, campaign posts, the assessment, and anything else that makes an
external factual assertion.

## What this file is

This is the curated, validated set of claim cards. Each card gives a writer the
exact sentence they may paste, the caveat that must travel with it, the source,
and the surfaces it may and may not appear on. A writer who has never opened the
evidence ledger can work entirely from this file.

**It supersedes `data/evidence/claim_cards.md` for all publication decisions.**
That raw file was imported before validation. Its cards state Verified and
Candidate findings as fact, carry figures that are no longer publishable, and
label several claims "Fact" that validation moved down. It is retained only for
provenance, so the record shows what the research package originally asserted and
what changed. Never copy language out of it. If the two files disagree, this one
wins, and the raw file is not edited to match.

The governing standard is `evidence-policy.md`. The gaps this library refuses to
fill are in `evidence-gaps.md`. The status column that decides everything here is
`Validated Status` in `evidence-ledger.csv`.

## The validated distribution

Forty-three claims went through the WS-19 validation pass on 2026-08-02:

| Validated status | Count | What it permits |
|---|---|---|
| Approved | 24 | Exact figures, counts, comparatives, and named findings may be published |
| Verified | 11 | Directional, attributed, qualified statements only. No figures |
| Candidate | 5 | Nothing. May guide internal research; never published |
| Rejected | 3 | Nothing, in any form |

Twenty-one claims moved status at validation. Ten rose to Approved once a
validator consulted the source and confirmed the claim text. Eleven fell, several
of them the exact claims the raw cards leaned on hardest. Every move is named on
the card that carries it.

## How to read a card

- **Status line.** The validated tier, stated plainly. Read it first.
- **Publication-ready language.** Paste this. Do not paraphrase upward. If you
  need a different sentence, the tier still governs the new one.
- **Caveat that must travel with it.** This appears in the same block of copy as
  the claim, not in a footnote at the bottom of the page. Policy rule 2.
- **Source, claim IDs, what changed at validation, where it may be used.**

## Two rules that close the file

1. **No card, no claim.** Any external factual assertion without a card in this
   file may not be published. If you find yourself wanting to state something
   about the world that no card covers, the options are to state it as our own
   practice observation and label it that way, or to leave it out. Inventing a
   citation to rescue a sentence is the one unforgivable move here.
2. **A surface not named is not approved.** If a card's "where it may be used"
   does not name the surface you are writing for, treat the claim as not cleared
   for it until the card says so.

## The two tests a writer runs

1. **The number-extraction test.** For any Tier B, Verified claim: could a reader
   pull a number out of this sentence? A percentage, a count, a sample size, a
   ratio, a rank? If yes, the sentence is a violation and must be rewritten.
   "Reported at its lowest level in that benchmark's own history" passes. "Fell to
   66.4 percent" does not.
2. **The tier-travel test.** A claim's tier belongs to the claim, not to the page.
   A Tier B claim stays directional in a playbook, on a landing page, in a post,
   and inside the assessment alike. There is no surface where the rule relaxes.

Structural numbers are exempt from all of this. Chapter counts, the day count in
a 90-day plan, step numbers, and counts of our own assets are not evidence
claims.

## Card C01. Writing productivity experiment

**Status line:** Tier A, Approved. Exact figures may be published.

**Publication-ready language:**

> In a preregistered experiment published in Science in 2023, access to ChatGPT
> reduced the average time college-educated professionals spent on mid-level
> professional writing tasks by about 40 percent and raised output quality by
> about 18 percent (Noy and Zhang, N=453).

Optional second sentence, also Approved, from U-AI-002:

> In the same experiment, performance gaps between workers narrowed rather than
> widened, and the workers who had been given access were about twice as likely to
> report using ChatGPT in their real job two weeks later, and about 1.6 times as
> likely two months later.

**Caveat that must travel with it:** These are controlled, occupation-specific
writing tasks, not full client engagements, regulated advice, or work performed
inside a confidentiality boundary. Quality was scored by evaluators on
experimental outputs, and the follow-up adoption figures are self-reported. This
is not a firm-level margin, realization, or utilization result.

**Source:** Noy, S. and Zhang, W., "Experimental evidence on the productivity
effects of generative artificial intelligence," Science, July 2023.
https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adh2586

**Claim IDs:** U-AI-001, U-AI-002

**What changed at validation:** No change at validation.

**Where it may be used:** Playbook chapters on task-level productivity and on
drafting workflows. Persona pages for Practice Leader, COO, and Managing Partner.
Campaign posts, with the caveat in the same post rather than a comment. Assessment
evidence callouts. It must NOT appear on an outcome page as a result this firm
produces, and it must NOT be attached to a client engagement, a fee, or a payback
period. The experiment measured a task; our outcome pages describe an engagement,
and the two are not the same object.

## Card C02. Consulting field experiment

**Status line:** Split card. U-AI-003 is Tier B, Verified: directional statements
only, no figures and no sample count. CONS-001 is DO NOT PUBLISH, Candidate.

**Publication-ready language (U-AI-003 only):**

> A preregistered field experiment run inside Boston Consulting Group found that
> on tasks sitting inside the model's capability range, consultants given access
> to GPT-4 completed more of the assigned work, completed it faster, and produced
> work rated higher in quality than a comparison group working without it
> (Dell'Acqua and colleagues, 2023).

**No publication-ready language for CONS-001.** None. This claim may not be
published in any form. CONS-001 is the framing that treats this single study as
the anchor evidence for AI in management consulting delivery, and it fell to
Candidate because one elite global firm cannot carry that weight for a mid-market
audience.

**Caveat that must travel with it:** The setting is one elite global firm, on
tasks built for the experiment, reported in a working paper produced with that
firm's participation. It does not transfer automatically to firms of 20 to 200
people, to multi-client confidentiality environments, or to work performed under
professional liability. It must always be paired with the frontier finding in
Card C03, because the same experiment produced both.

**Source:** Dell'Acqua, McFowland, Mollick, Lifshitz-Assaf, Kellogg, Rajendran,
Krayer, Candelon and Lakhani, "Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier,"
HBS and BCG working paper, September 2023.
https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4573321

**Claim IDs:** U-AI-003 (Verified), CONS-001 (Candidate)

**What changed at validation:** U-AI-003 was approved at import and fell to
Verified, on the limitation recorded in the ledger that the study covers a single
elite global firm with experiment-designed tasks and is a working paper rather
than a peer-reviewed journal article. CONS-001 was approved at import and fell two
steps to Candidate, on the recorded limitation that mid-market consulting is
untested at this rigor.

**Where it may be used:** Playbooks and persona pages, as the directional sentence
above with its caveat and with Card C03 adjacent. Campaign posts, directional
only. It must NOT appear in the management consulting playbook as the anchor
claim, which is what CONS-001 was for and why CONS-001 is unpublishable. It must
NOT appear on any outcome page, in the assessment scoring logic, or in a business
case as a productivity input. The figures from this study appear nowhere in the
library, including in charts, pull quotes, and social graphics.

## Card C03. Jagged technological frontier

**Status line:** Tier B, Verified. Directional statements only. The finding
carries no figures to begin with, so the constraint here is attribution and
qualification, not arithmetic.

**Publication-ready language:**

> Researchers studying AI use inside a large consulting firm describe what they
> call a jagged technological frontier: generative AI lifts performance on some
> knowledge tasks while degrading the quality of work on other tasks of similar
> apparent difficulty that fall outside the model's capability, particularly when
> professionals defer to it rather than checking it (Dell'Acqua and colleagues,
> 2023).

The argument this supports, which may be stated as our own reasoning rather than
as a finding:

> The boundary is not visible from inside the task, it is specific to the model
> and the domain, and it moves as models change. That is why accountable human
> review is a permanent control in professional work rather than a temporary
> scaffold to be removed once the tools improve. A firm cannot govern a boundary
> it cannot see, so it governs the review instead.

**Caveat that must travel with it:** Where the frontier falls is specific to the
model, the prompt pattern, and the domain, and it shifts over time. The finding
comes from a working paper studying one firm in the GPT-4 era. The second
paragraph above is our inference from the finding, not part of the study's
results.

**Source:** Dell'Acqua and colleagues (HBS and BCG), "Navigating the Jagged
Technological Frontier," September 2023.
https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4573321

**Claim IDs:** U-AI-004

**What changed at validation:** Approved at import, fell to Verified, on the
recorded limitation that the frontier boundary is model-specific and
task-specific and shifts over time, and that the source is a working paper tied to
GPT-4 era capabilities.

**Where it may be used:** This is the most load-bearing Verified card in the
library and it may be used widely. Playbook governance and quality chapters.
Persona pages for Risk and General Counsel, Practice Leader, COO, and CIO.
Assessment items about review gates and task routing. Campaign posts. It must NOT
be restated with the productivity findings from the same study, which are
Verified and may be published only without figures under Card C02. It must NOT be used to claim that our review
design has been validated, only that the risk it addresses has been documented.

## Card C04. Support-agent productivity and novice gains

**Status line:** Split card. U-AI-005 is DO NOT PUBLISH, Candidate: the study
figures may not appear in any form. IT-001, the managed-services transfer
hypothesis drawn from the same study, is Tier B, Verified, and can carry a
directional version.

**Publication-ready language for U-AI-005:** None. This claim may not be published
in any form. It fell to Candidate because the setting is customer support chat at
a single software company with agents largely outside the United States, which is
too far from professional advisory work to support a stated figure aimed at
professional services firms.

**Publication-ready language for IT-001 (Verified, directional):**

> In a study of customer support agents at a software company, researchers
> reported that access to a generative AI assistant raised the number of issues
> agents resolved per hour, with the gains concentrated among the least
> experienced agents and little measurable change for the most experienced
> (Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond, NBER working paper). For a managed services
> provider or an IT service desk, that is a hypothesis worth testing on your own
> ticket data, not evidence about your desk.

That sentence genuinely works because IT-001 was validated as a labelled transfer
hypothesis rather than as a direct measurement, and Verified permits a
directional, attributed statement. It does not license the figures back into the
copy by another route.

**Caveat that must travel with it:** The origin setting is software support chat,
with most agents outside the United States, reported in a working paper. Agents
could accept or ignore the tool's suggestions. Applying it to a managed services
desk is an inference, not a finding, and ticket mix, service levels, and
multi-tenant client confidentiality all differ. It is not evidence about legal,
audit, or consulting advice, and it is not evidence about billable margin.

**Source:** Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond, "Generative AI at Work," NBER Working
Paper 31161, April 2023, revised November 2023.
https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w31161/w31161.pdf

**Claim IDs:** U-AI-005 (Candidate), IT-001 (Verified)

**What changed at validation:** U-AI-005 was approved at import and fell two steps
to Candidate, on the recorded limitation that the setting is customer support
chat with primarily non-United States agents and does not cover professional
liability contexts. IT-001 was approved at import and fell to Verified, on the
recorded limitation that the transfer to managed services is inference rather
than direct evidence.

**Read this before using IT-001.** IT-001 and U-AI-005 are the same study under
two claim IDs. IT-001's Verified status attaches to the transfer framing, not to
the measurement, and the measurement itself is Candidate. So the permitted
sentence may set up the hypothesis and must never present the origin finding as
an established result, and no figure from that study may re-enter copy through
this route. A Verified wrapper around a Candidate finding is a structural
weakness in the ledger rather than a property of the evidence; it is recorded
here and flagged for the next validation pass rather than resolved by writing.

**Where it may be used:** The IT-001 directional sentence may be used in the MSP
and IT consulting playbook, on the COO and CIO persona pages, and in assessment
items that frame a hypothesis to test. It must carry the transfer label every
time. It must NOT be used as a general professional services productivity claim,
because IT-001's validated scope is the service desk transfer specifically. It
must NOT appear in the legal, accounting, architecture, engineering, or executive
search packs. Nothing from U-AI-005 appears anywhere. See gap G-06, which is why
the MSP playbook teaches seat economics qualitatively.

## Card C05. Experience-gap compression pattern

**Status line:** Tier B, Verified. Directional statements only, no figures and no
sample counts. This was always an inference across studies; validation confirmed
it as a pattern, not a measurement.

**Publication-ready language:**

> Across several causal studies of generative AI on knowledge work published in
> 2023, the measured gains concentrated among less experienced or lower baseline
> performers rather than spreading evenly across everyone (Noy and Zhang in
> Science; Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond at NBER; Dell'Acqua and colleagues in the
> consulting field experiment). Read together, they suggest that on tasks inside
> model capability, AI assistance can compress part of the gap that experience
> normally supplies. That is an argument for treating AI as an onboarding and
> knowledge-reuse system rather than as a headcount question.

**Caveat that must travel with it:** This is a cross-study pattern, not a single
study, and the three studies used different tasks and different populations. None
of them studied a professional services firm's onboarding. It does not show that
AI substitutes for professional judgment, licensure, supervision, or partner
review, and it is not evidence of firm-level margin improvement.

**Source:** Synthesis across Noy and Zhang, Science, 2023
(https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adh2586); Brynjolfsson, Li and
Raymond, NBER w31161, 2023
(https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w31161/w31161.pdf); and
Dell'Acqua and colleagues, 2023
(https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4573321). The ledger records
the Science paper as the primary URL for this synthesis claim.

**Claim IDs:** U-KNOW-001

**What changed at validation:** Approved at import, fell to Verified, on the
ledger's own note that this is a strategic synthesis whose underlying tasks and
populations differ and which does not prove onboarding return in a professional
services firm.

**Where it may be used:** The playbook chapter on knowledge leverage and reuse,
labelled as an inference in the copy itself. Persona pages for Practice Leader,
COO, and Managing Partner. Assessment items about onboarding time to
productivity. It must NOT be published with any of the three studies' figures
attached, and it must NOT be used to support a stated onboarding return, payback
period, or ramp-time reduction. See gap G-08: reuse is argued from the operating
mechanism, and this pattern is the supporting inference, not the proof.

## Card C06. Professional services economics benchmark

**Status line:** Tier B, Verified. Directional statements only. No utilization
figure, no margin figure, no growth figure, no firm count, no target percentage.

**Publication-ready language:**

> Surveyed billable utilization across professional services firms has been
> reported at its lowest level in that benchmark's own history and below the
> benchmark's own stated target, while margins held roughly flat year over year

attributed to AI, and the survey does not measure it.

**Source:** SPI Research Professional Services Maturity Benchmark, as summarized
by Deltek, 2026 report covering 2025 performance.
https://www.deltek.com/resources/articles/professional-services-benchmarks/

**Claim IDs:** U-ECON-001, U-ECON-002, U-ECON-003

**What changed at validation:** No change at validation. All three were Verified
at import as well, which means the raw card's figures were never publishable under
the policy, and any existing copy carrying them is a violation to be rewritten to
the language above.

**Where it may be used:** The playbook chapter on professional services economics.
Persona pages for COO, CFO, and Managing Partner. The assessment, as framing for
why a firm should establish its own baseline. Campaign posts, directional only. It
must NOT be used as the assessment's numeric baseline, must NOT be charted, and
must NOT appear as a benchmark a client is measured against. When a firm needs a
utilization number, it comes from that firm's own delivery data, not from here.
The phrasing "lowest level in that benchmark's own history" is expressly sanctioned
by the policy's worked example; do not extend that licence to any other figure.

## Card C07. Adoption without scale

**Status line:** Split card. U-ADOPT-001 through U-ADOPT-004 are Tier A, Approved:
exact figures may be published. U-MEAS-001, the sentence that binds them into a
single pattern, is Tier B, Verified: the synthesis must be stated without figures.

**Publication-ready language, Approved, figures permitted:**

> McKinsey's 2025 State of AI survey reports that 88 percent of respondents use AI
> regularly in at least one business function, up from 78 percent a year earlier,
> while nearly two thirds say their organizations have not yet begun scaling AI
> across the enterprise.

> In the same survey, 23 percent report scaling an agentic AI system somewhere in
> the enterprise and a further 39 percent are experimenting with agents, but no
> more than 10 percent report scaled agents in any given business function. And
> while 64 percent say AI is enabling innovation, only 39 percent report EBIT
> impact at the enterprise level.

> Deloitte's fourth-wave State of Generative AI survey found that more than two
> thirds of respondents expected 30 percent or fewer of their generative AI
> experiments to be fully scaled within three to six months, that 78 percent
> expected to increase AI spending in the coming fiscal year, and that regulatory
> compliance had risen from 28 percent of respondents in the first wave to 38
> percent in the fourth as a leading barrier.

**Publication-ready language, Verified, no figures:**

> Two independently run surveys point the same way. Routine AI use is now common
> in at least one business function, while enterprise-wide scaling and reported
> profit impact lag well behind it, and most generative AI experiments are not
> expected to reach full scale in the near term (McKinsey State of AI; Deloitte
> State of Generative AI).

Use the Approved paragraphs when you want the numbers. Use the Verified paragraph
when you want the thesis. Do not merge them into a sentence that asserts the
pattern and the figures as one validated finding.

**Caveat that must travel with it:** Both are self-reported surveys, skewed toward
large organizations, published by firms that sell AI services. Neither is
professional services specific, neither is limited to firms of 20 to 200 people,
and self-reported EBIT impact is not audited financial impact. Regular use in one
function is not value realized.

**Source:** McKinsey and QuantumBlack, The State of AI, global survey 2025,
N=1,993 across 105 nations, fielded June to July 2025.
https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai
Deloitte, State of Generative AI Q4 press release, January 2025, survey fielded
July to September 2024, N=2,773.
https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/about/press-room/state-of-generative-ai.html

**Claim IDs:** U-ADOPT-001, U-ADOPT-002, U-ADOPT-003, U-ADOPT-004 (Approved);
U-MEAS-001 (Verified)

**What changed at validation:** All four adoption claims were verified at import
and rose to Approved when the WS-19 pass consulted the sources and confirmed the
claim text against them. U-MEAS-001 moved the other way: approved at import, fell
to Verified, because it is a pattern assembled across two self-reported
consultancy surveys rather than a measured finding, and neither survey is specific
to professional services.

**Where it may be used:** This is the evidentiary spine of the argument that
governance and workflow redesign beat tool pilots. Playbook opening chapters and
the measurement chapter. Persona pages for CIO, CFO, COO, and Managing Partner.
Campaign posts, with the sponsorship caveat in the same post. Assessment framing.
It must NOT be presented as professional services data, and the figures must NOT
be applied to a specific firm's likelihood of scaling. The Verified synthesis
sentence must NOT acquire numbers on its way into a headline or a graphic.

## Card C09. ABA Formal Opinion 512 duties

**Status line:** Tier A, Approved. Exact quotations and attributions may be
published.

**Publication-ready language:**

> ABA Formal Opinion 512, issued July 29, 2024, states that lawyers using
> generative AI tools must fully consider their applicable ethical obligations,
> including the duties to provide competent representation, to protect client
> information, to communicate with clients, to supervise employees and agents, to
> advance only meritorious claims and contentions, to ensure candor toward the
> tribunal, and to charge reasonable fees.

> The same opinion is explicit that lawyers need not become experts in generative
> AI. They must have a reasonable understanding of the capabilities and
> limitations of the specific tool they are using.

**Caveat that must travel with it:** The opinion interprets the ABA Model Rules.
State adoption and state ethics opinions vary, and the reasonableness standard is
fact-specific. This is guidance, not statute, and nothing here is legal advice for
a particular firm or matter.

**Source:** ABA Standing Committee on Ethics and Professional Responsibility,
Formal Opinion 512, Generative Artificial Intelligence Tools, July 29, 2024.
https://www.americanbar.org/content/dam/aba/administrative/professional_responsibility/ethics-opinions/aba-formal-opinion-512.pdf

**Claim IDs:** LAW-001, LAW-002

**What changed at validation:** No change at validation.

**Where it may be used:** The law firm playbook, as the core authority. The Risk
and General Counsel persona page. The legal module of the assessment. Campaign
posts. Outcome pages, where it establishes the duty our controls are designed
against, provided the copy does not imply the opinion endorses any product or
approach. It must NOT be paraphrased into a claim about how often firms are
disciplined for AI misuse; the opinion quantifies nothing.

## Card C21. Measure operations, not model demos

**Status line:** STRATEGIC JUDGMENT, NOT EVIDENCE. This card has no claim ID, no
source, and no tier, because it is not a finding. It is our recommended
measurement architecture. It may be published as our position, in our voice, and
it may never be cited as a research result.

**Publication-ready language:**

> Our position is that the defensible management system for AI value in a

**Caveat that must travel with it:** This is a recommended architecture, not an
empirical finding. No study in our evidence library establishes that this set of

industry, and the baseline has to come from the firm's own delivery and pursuit
data.

**Source:** None. This is a design choice informed by, but not derived from, the

(Cards C09, C10, C14, C15).

**Claim IDs:** None. Do not attach one. If a writer needs a claim ID here, the
sentence they are writing is the wrong sentence.

**What changed at validation:** Not applicable. This was never a ledger claim and
was never validated. The raw card listed supporting sources in a way that could be
misread as derivation, which is why this card states the label first.

**Where it may be used:** Assessment methodology, the CFO playbook, outcome pages
describing how we measure, and internal positioning. It must always be written in
first person or otherwise marked as our recommendation. It must NOT appear in a
sources block, a research summary, an evidence appendix, or a citation, and it

## Maintenance

- A claim moves tier only through a recorded validation event: validator, date,
  source consulted, and what was confirmed. Changing a status in the ledger without
  that record is prohibited, and changing it here without changing it in the
  ledger is worse.
- When a status changes, update the card, the status line, the publication
  language, and the "what changed at validation" line together. A card with a new
  tier and old language is the most dangerous object this system can produce.
- When a gap closes, record it in `evidence-gaps.md` with the claim ID that closed
  it, and add or update the card here.
- Never edit `data/evidence/claim_cards.md`. It is the provenance record of what
  was originally imported, including the parts that turned out to be wrong.
