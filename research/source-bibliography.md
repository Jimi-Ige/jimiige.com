# Source bibliography

Every source behind the evidence ledger, grouped by tier. Tier order is the
preference order from the Content Evidence and Production System: government,
regulators, standards bodies and professional associations first; then academic;
then issuer primary; then labs; then consultancy and vendor material, which may
support framing and hypotheses but never stands alone as proof of a material claim.

Each entry lists the claims it supports and the limit of what it can support.
Statuses shown are statuses at import. Validated statuses live in
`evidence-ledger.csv`; `evidence-policy.md` defines what each status licenses.

## Tier 2: Academic research and transparent working papers

**Experimental evidence on the productivity effects of generative artificial intelligence**  
Shakked Noy; Whitney Zhang (MIT) , Science July 2023 (Science 381)  
<https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adh2586>  
Claims: U-AI-001, U-AI-002, U-KNOW-001  
Evidence type: Causal (RCT), Causal (RCT) + descriptive follow-up, Inference from multiple causal studies  
Import status: approved  
Limit: Controlled writing tasks, not full client engagements; short horizon; geography not fully specified; quality graded by evaluators on task outputs; results may not generalize to regulated professional advice, multi-party client work, or firm systems with confidentiality constraints. Causal within experiment design.

**Generative AI at Work (NBER Working Paper 31161)**  
Brynjolfsson, Li, Raymond , NBER April 2023; revised November 2023  
<https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w31161/w31161.pdf>  
Claims: IT-001, U-AI-005  
Evidence type: Causal (quasi-experimental DiD), Causal in origin setting; inference for MSP  
Import status: approved  
Limit: Customer support chat, not client-facing professional advisory work; geography primarily non-U.S. agents; working paper; tool suggested responses agents could accept/ignore (~38% adherence); does not measure professional liability contexts. Quasi-experimental causal design.

**Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier: Field Experimental Evidence of the Effects of AI on Knowledge Worker Productivity and Quality**  
Dell'Acqua, McFowland, Mollick, Lifshitz-Assaf, Kellogg, Rajendran, Krayer, Candelon, Lakhani (HBS / BCG) September 2023 (SSRN / HBS)  
<https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4573321>  
Claims: CONS-001, U-AI-003, U-AI-004  
Evidence type: Causal, Causal (field RCT)  
Import status: approved  
Limit: Single elite global firm (BCG) , limited generalizability to mid-market 20-200 employee firms; tasks designed for experiment; 'outside the frontier' tasks showed quality degradation when AI used inappropriately (jagged frontier); BCG/HBS collaboration; quality metric is study-defined. Causal within design.
