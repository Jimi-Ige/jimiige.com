AI use you could explain to the client who handed you the keys.

The risk is not that the firm uses AI. It is that the firm cannot say where, inside which boundaries, and with whom accountable. Governed AI operations replaces improvisation with a posture you can show: credentials vaulted, context walled by client, a named engineer answering for every consequential action, evidence that reconstructs what happened, and a disclosure answer ready before the questionnaire arrives.

When this becomes urgent

The moments that put it on your desk.

Client contracts and compliance overlays now speak to AI directly, and the firm's posture varies by whoever is asked.
A near-miss surfaced: a credential or a client detail in a tool nobody approved, caught by luck rather than by design.
Two clients in the same market are served by the same engineers, and nothing technical keeps their environments out of the same context.
An insurer, an auditor, or a client asked about AI controls in a privileged environment, and the answer was assembled overnight.

What AI changes

The work moves. The accountability does not.

What changes is how fast retrieval and drafting happen. What never changes is who answers for a change to a client environment.

Every AI-assisted resolution is traceable to its sources and its approver, so questions get answers instead of investigations.

The firm's posture becomes one sentence you control, instead of a hundred improvisations you discover later.

The controls that make it trustworthy
Credential vaulting, with auditing for the day a secret reaches a prompt, an output, or a log.
Client-walled context, checked against each client's contract and compliance overlay, because the regimes genuinely differ.
No standing permission for AI to change a client environment, at any maturity stage, in any workflow.
Retention rules for prompts and outputs set deliberately, including what is destroyed at offboarding.
An incident path with rehearsed client communication, because the plan you need is the one written before the miss.

What the published evidence says

Quality risk from misapplied AI is documented rather than hypothetical: a field experiment reported professional performance degrading when AI was relied on for tasks outside its capability frontier.

Dell'Acqua et al., 2023 (SSRN)

The study ran with consultants on business tasks, not engineers with privileged access, and frontier boundaries move as models and domains change. On a service desk the outside-frontier failure mode is a plausible-sounding runbook action against a live client system, which argues for task routing and change approval rather than prohibition; prohibition only relocates the work to shadow tools.
