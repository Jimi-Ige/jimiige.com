Executive Search · Managing Partner / CEO

Grow the firm without betting it on the market for proven consultants.

Your growth plan currently has one engine: hire consultants whose networks arrive with them, and the market rarely sells one at a price the model affords. Governed AI adds a second engine. Market mapping, drafting, assembly, and reporting move onto rails, consultant attention returns to candidates, clients, and judgment, and each desk runs more searches well without the firm's name or its network paying for it.

When this becomes urgent

The moments that put it on your desk.

The growth plan assumes hiring proven consultants with portable networks, and every year the market proves it will not supply them at that price.
Consultants are at their search ceiling while mandate demand keeps arriving, and the choice reads as decline work or let coverage and cadence slip.
The firm's market knowledge lives in a few heads and a database nobody fully trusts, and succession is a hope rather than a plan.
Clients have started asking how the firm uses AI and how candidate data is protected, and the answer depends on who they ask.
The workflows that move it

Where the leverage is, for your chair.

1.Pitch-to-Mandate. Win more of the right searches without more partner evenings: the take-or-decline call stays senior, assembling the track record stops being senior work.
2.Research-to-Longlist. Markets covered faster without narrowing the lens, starting from the firm's prior work in the territory instead of from zero.
3.Search-to-Client Reporting. A portfolio view you can run the firm on: every search's status traceable to its live record, not to who you asked.

What AI changes

The work moves. The accountability does not.

Mapping, research assembly, and first drafts come off consultant desks, so senior hours concentrate on candidates, clients, and the verdict.

Every pitch and every search starts from the firm's accumulated market knowledge, with provenance, instead of from a blank page.

The operating picture of every desk assembles itself from live search records instead of consuming a partner meeting to reconstruct.

The controls that make it trustworthy
Mandate-level boundaries, so one search's confidences never surface in another's work uninvited.
A consultant reads, edits, and owns every word that reaches a candidate or a client, unchanged from today.
The verdict stays human by explicit rule: the read on a person, every reference, and every reject decision belong to the consultant.
Retention for candidate data written deliberately, so what the firm holds is what it decided to hold.
A disclosure answer written before clients ask, one a managing partner would happily read aloud.
Proof, not promises

What we would baseline and measure.

No borrowed benchmarks and no invented results. These are the numbers your own pilot would stand on:

Searches active per consultant without quality slippage
Days from mandate signature to a client-ready longlist
Client update cadence kept versus promised
Placement stick rate after twelve months
What the published evidence says

Enterprise surveys in 2025 find regular AI use near nine in ten organizations, while only a minority report enterprise-level earnings impact and most experiments never fully scale.

McKinsey State of AI 2025; Deloitte generative-AI survey 2025

Self-reported, large-enterprise-skewed consultancy surveys, none of them about search firms. The pattern argues for operating discipline and measurement, not for urgency theater; no comparable primary evidence exists for executive search economics.
