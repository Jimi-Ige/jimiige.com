Executive Search · Risk / Data Privacy Lead

Candidate data handling your firm could explain to its most demanding client.

The risk is not that the firm uses AI. It is that the firm cannot say where candidate data goes, how long it is kept, or whether a model ever influenced who left a list. Governed AI operations replaces improvisation with a defensible posture: mandate boundaries in both directions, human verdicts by explicit rule, retention decided rather than defaulted, and a disclosure answer ready before anyone asks.

When this becomes urgent

The moments that put it on your desk.

A near-miss surfaced: candidate notes or a client spec in an unapproved tool, caught by luck rather than by design.
A client asked how candidate data is protected, and the answer had to be assembled overnight.
Retention has never been decided, so candidate files accumulate by default, including notes on people who never asked to be in them.
Nobody can say with confidence whether software ever influenced a reject decision, and the regulatory exposure runs in both directions.
The workflows that move it

Where the leverage is, for your chair.

1.Assessment Judgment & Reference Integrity. Models may organize interview material; the verdict, every reference, and every reject decision stay with the consultant, by written rule.
2.Candidate & Client Communication. Every outbound word owned by a consultant, with discretion rules that hold at machine speed as well as human speed.
3.Research-to-Longlist. Research from approved sources with provenance and recorded reasons for every cut, which is the audit trail of the lens.

What AI changes

The work moves. The accountability does not.

What changes is assembly speed. What never changes is accountability: a named consultant answers for every verdict and every send.

Every AI-assisted artifact is traceable to its sources and its owner, so questions get answers instead of investigations.

The firm's posture becomes one sentence you control, instead of a hundred improvisations you discover.

The controls that make it trustworthy
Mandate-level boundaries protecting both confidences at once: what candidates said, and what the spec reveals about the client.
Retention and deletion for candidate data written deliberately: what is kept when a search closes, for how long, and what is destroyed.
The verdict human by explicit rule: no model makes a reject decision, and references remain unquoted conversations between people.
An incident path with rehearsed client and candidate communication, written before the miss instead of during it.
A standing disclosure answer, reviewed by you, consistent everywhere it is given.
Proof, not promises

What the published evidence says

The U.S. EEOC and Department of Justice have warned that employers' use of AI and automated software tools in hiring, monitoring, pay, or promotion decisions can violate the Americans with Disabilities Act.

U.S. EEOC and Department of Justice

A warning and technical assistance, not a finding that any specific tool is unlawful, and not legal advice. A search firm sits on both sides of it, screening in its own process and advising clients who screen in theirs, which is why reject decisions stay human.
