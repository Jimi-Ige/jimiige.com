One governed platform, instead of a sprawl holding fragments of the network.

The desks are already using AI, approved or not, and some of it is touching candidate data. The question on your desk is whether that becomes a platform the firm can operate and defend, or a sprawl it gets blamed for. Governed AI operations gives you the architecture: mandate-scoped access, approved sources, provenance and retention by default, and a sanctioned path fast enough that nobody wants the shadow one.

When this becomes urgent

The moments that put it on your desk.

Shadow AI is already in the building: individual subscriptions and browser tools, and nobody can say what candidate data has touched them.
Desks and sector teams are picking point tools independently, each holding fragments of the network with no shared control model.
Client security and data questionnaires have started asking AI questions the current estate cannot answer cleanly.
Leadership wants AI rolled out, and technology is expected to make it safe after the fact.
The workflows that move it

Where the leverage is, for your chair.

1.Research-to-Longlist. Research runs on an approved source list with provenance attached, so every profile is traceable and the corpus has an owner.
2.Search-to-Client Reporting. One live record per search as the single source of synthesis, which is an architecture decision before it is a reporting one.
3.Candidate & Client Communication. Drafting and scheduling inside the governed platform, so candidate data stops leaking into personal tools.

The full treatment is in the playbook: Run More Searches with Governed AI.

What AI changes

The work moves. The accountability does not.

The sanctioned path becomes the fastest path, which is the only adoption strategy that has ever beaten shadow IT.

Access follows the search team automatically, so mandate boundaries are enforced by the platform, not by policy documents.

Every AI-assisted artifact carries its source trail, so client questionnaires get answered from the system instead of from archaeology.

The controls that make it trustworthy
Access mirroring mandate staffing, provisioned and revoked with the search team.
An approved-tool list with a real intake path, so new needs have somewhere to go besides the shadows.
Retention and deletion rules for candidate data as platform defaults, not per-desk habits.
An incident path that assumes a miss will eventually happen and names who does what when it does.
Proof, not promises

What we would baseline and measure.

No borrowed benchmarks and no invented results. These are the numbers your own pilot would stand on:

Mandates with candidate data confined to sanctioned tools, versus observed shadow use
Turnaround time on client security and candidate-data questionnaires
Access reviews confirming rights mirror live search-team staffing, passed without remediation
Time from tool request to governed availability
What the published evidence says

NIST's AI Risk Management Framework (January 2023) and its Generative AI Profile (July 2024) give a voluntary, vendor-neutral scaffold for governing AI design, deployment, use, and evaluation.

NIST AI RMF 1.0; NIST.AI.600-1

Voluntary frameworks, not mandates and not a safe harbor. They still have to be adapted to candidate-data obligations, cross-border rules, and each client's expectations of discretion.
