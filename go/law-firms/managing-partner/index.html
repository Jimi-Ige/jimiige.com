What AI changes

The work moves. The accountability does not.

Research, first drafts, and retrieval come off senior desks, so partner hours concentrate on positions, clients, and judgment.

The firm's accumulated work product becomes an asset the whole firm can draw on, walled by client and matter.

The operating picture of the firm assembles itself from live sources instead of consuming a layer of management time.

The controls that make it trustworthy
Client and matter boundaries mirroring ethical screens, so one client's confidences never surface in another's work.
Attorney review and sign-off before anything leaves the firm, unchanged from today, so accountability never moves.
Verification of every cited authority by the attorney, written into the workflow rather than assumed.
A standing AI answer, checked against client guidelines, that you would happily give your most demanding client.

What the published evidence says

ABA Formal Opinion 512 (July 2024) sets out the duties a lawyer using generative AI must fully consider: competence, confidentiality, communication, supervision, meritorious claims, candor toward the tribunal, and reasonable fees. It sets the competence bar where a firm can operationalize it: a reasonable understanding of the specific tool's capabilities and limits, not becoming an AI expert.

ABA Formal Opinion 512, July 2024

The opinion interprets the ABA Model Rules; state adoption and state opinions vary, and nothing here is legal advice. It defines the duties, not the operating model. Building the operating model is the growth decision on your desk.
