An AI investment case built for a firm that bills by the hour.

You have seen the vendor math: hours saved multiplied by a blended rate, payback by the second month. In an hourly firm that arithmetic is not just optimistic, it is confused, because a saved hour is not revenue until someone decides where it lands. The governed version is different by construction: baselines before pilots, program cost counted once, adoption lag priced in, ranges instead of points, and freed capacity named to a destination, more matters, alternative-fee margin, client development, before it is counted as anything.

AI assists operations and documentation. Attorneys retain every legal judgment, every client position, and every signature. Nothing in this content is legal advice.

What AI changes

The work moves. The accountability does not.

The cost side consolidates: one program cost, counted once, with each workflow carrying only its marginal cost.

The value side gets honest: baselined workflows, measured deltas, and freed hours assigned to a destination in the fee structure that prices them.

Every number in the case traces to a source, because in a firm that sells rigor the business case is a work sample.

The controls that make it trustworthy
Baselines captured before any pilot, so every claim has a before.
One owner and one source of record per metric.
Adoption lag modeled explicitly, so value arriving late was planned, not excused.
Ranges with stated assumptions in front of the partnership, never false points.
Proof, not promises

What we would baseline and measure.

No borrowed benchmarks and no invented results. These are the numbers your own pilot would stand on:

Program cost, total and per active workflow
Write-offs and write-downs by matter type
Matter-budget variance during the matter
Measured first-draft and pitch turnaround deltas against baseline
What the published evidence says

In Thomson Reuters' 2024 Future of Professionals survey (N=2,205 across legal, tax and accounting, risk, and the C-suite), professionals predicted AI could save about four hours per week within a year and twelve within five years. Those figures are expectations respondents reported, not observed savings.

Thomson Reuters Future of Professionals, 2024

Vendor-sponsored survey of predictions, not a time-motion study. In an hourly firm a predicted hour is doubly hypothetical: it may never be saved, and if saved it is not margin until the firm decides where it lands. Only a baseline and honest measurement can show what actually happened.
