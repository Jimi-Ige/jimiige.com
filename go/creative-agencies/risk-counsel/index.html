AI use the agency could explain to its most demanding client.

The risk is not that the agency uses AI. It is that the agency cannot say how, where, and under what controls, on paper that now differs client by client. Governed AI operations replaces improvisation with a posture you set: account walls that mirror team assignment, human creative direction documented on every deliverable, a usage-rights position checked against each contract, and one disclosure answer given the same way in every room.

When this becomes urgent

The moments that put it on your desk.

Client contracts have started speaking to AI directly, and the agency's own paper is silent on it.

Three people from the same agency gave three different answers to one client's question about AI.

What AI changes

The work moves. The accountability does not.

What changes is assembly speed. What never changes is accountability: a named human owns every consequential output that leaves the agency.

Human creative direction is documented on every deliverable, which supports the rights posture and the disclosure answer with a single habit.

The agency's position becomes one sentence you control, instead of a hundred improvisations you discover afterwards.

The controls that make it trustworthy
Account-level data and retrieval boundaries checked against each client's actual contract, because contracts now differ on AI.
Internal creative review and a named approver before anything client-facing, with the check recorded rather than assumed.
Human creative direction documented on every deliverable, kept with the job file.
Retention rules for prompts, outputs, and reference sets, set deliberately at job close rather than defaulted by a tool.
One written disclosure answer, reviewed by you, given the same way by every account team.

What the published evidence says

In January 2025 the US Copyright Office concluded that questions of copyrightability and AI can be addressed under existing law, and that using AI tools to assist rather than replace human creativity does not, by itself, deny copyright protection for the original human expression in a work.

US Copyright Office, Copyright and Artificial Intelligence, Part 2, January 2025

Agency legal analysis, not a court holding. It leaves training-data and infringement questions to other parts of the Office's work and to ongoing litigation, and nothing here is legal advice for client contracts or the agency's own IP policy. The operational lesson is narrow and useful: protection attaches to the human contribution, which makes documenting human creative direction a habit worth building.
