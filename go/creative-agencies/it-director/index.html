What AI changes

The work moves. The accountability does not.

The sanctioned path becomes the fastest path, which is the only strategy that has ever beaten shadow tooling in a creative studio.

Access follows team assignment, so account walls are enforced by the platform instead of by anyone's memory of who is on what.

Every AI-assisted asset carries its source trail, so client questionnaires and rights questions get answered from the system instead of from archaeology.

The controls that make it trustworthy
Account-scoped access, provisioned and revoked with team assignment rather than reviewed once a year.
An approved-tool list with a real intake path for new needs, so exceptions have somewhere to go besides the shadows.
Provenance, rights status, and retention as platform defaults, not per-team practice.
An incident path that assumes a miss will eventually happen and names who does what when it does.

What the published evidence says

Enterprise surveys in 2025 put regular AI use at 88 percent of organizations in at least one function, while nearly two-thirds have not begun enterprise-wide scaling and most experiments never fully scale.

McKinsey State of AI 2025; Deloitte generative-AI survey 2025

Self-reported consultancy surveys, skewed to large enterprises and produced by firms that sell AI services. Not agency data. Read the shape, not the benchmark: AI use spreads through an organization faster than any platform is built to hold it, which is precisely the estate you inherit when the sanctioned path is not the fast one.
