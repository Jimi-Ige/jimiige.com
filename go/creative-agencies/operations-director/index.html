What AI changes

The work moves. The accountability does not.

Assembly leaves the critical path: research, first drafts, versions, and status synthesis run on rails instead of on people.

Handoffs become records instead of conversations, so nothing promised in the pitch room goes missing on the way to production.

Revision and scope signals surface the week they diverge from what the scope assumed, while the job can still be saved.

The controls that make it trustworthy
Account-scoped access that mirrors team assignment, so each team reaches exactly what it should and nothing from the account across the aisle.
Feedback consolidated with conflicts preserved and attributed, never averaged into a position nobody actually holds.
Internal creative review before any external route, with no exception for small deliverables, because clients do not grade on size.
Scope changes logged as dated events with the request attached, so the creep conversation has evidence instead of grievance.
Retention and reuse rules set at job close, while the boundaries are still fresh.

What the published evidence says

In a preregistered experiment published in Science, access to a general AI assistant cut time on occupation-specific professional writing tasks by about 40 percent and raised evaluator-judged quality by about 18 percent, across roughly 453 college-educated professionals.

Noy & Zhang, Science, 2023

Controlled writing tasks, evaluator-scored on experimental outputs, not full client engagements and not creative concepting. It is production-adjacent evidence, not a firm-level margin or realization result. Aim it at the mechanical layer of the studio, where it actually applies, and baseline your own workflows before believing anything about them.
