An AI investment case that survives a partner meeting in a seasonal business.

You have seen the vendor math: hours saved multiplied by a standard rate, payback by the second month, benefits counted in three business cases at once. In a firm whose year is decided in a few compressed months, that arithmetic is not merely optimistic, it is confused, because a March hour and a July hour are not the same asset. The governed version is different by construction: baselines before pilots, program cost counted once, adoption lag priced in, ranges instead of points, and freed capacity named to a destination before anyone books it as a benefit.

AI assists operations and documentation. Accountable licensed professionals retain every judgment and every sign-off. Nothing in this content is tax, accounting, or audit advice.

What AI changes

The work moves. The accountability does not.

The cost side consolidates: one program cost, counted once, with each workflow carrying only its marginal cost.

The value side gets honest: baselined workflows, deltas measured across a full season, and freed hours assigned to a named destination.

Every number in the case traces to a source, because in a firm that sells rigor the business case is a work sample.

The controls that make it trustworthy
Baselines captured before any pilot, and taken against a real season rather than a quiet month.
One owner and one source of record per metric.
Adoption lag modeled explicitly, so value arriving after the first season was planned rather than excused.
Ranges with stated assumptions in front of the partnership, never false points.
Nothing in the case assumes AI carries professional judgment: AI assists operations and documentation, the licensed professional keeps every judgment and every sign-off, and the review hierarchy stays fully funded in the cost base.
Proof, not promises

What we would baseline and measure.

No borrowed benchmarks and no invented results. These are the numbers your own pilot would stand on:

Program cost, total and per active workflow
Realization by client and by service line
Write-downs by engagement, read against when they first became visible
Measured deltas against baseline on the piloted workflows, read across a full season
What the published evidence says

In McKinsey's 2025 State of AI survey (N=1,993), 64 percent of respondents said AI is enabling innovation while only 39 percent reported EBIT impact at the enterprise level.

McKinsey / QuantumBlack, The State of AI, 2025

A global cross-industry consultancy survey, self-reported perception rather than audited financials, with large-organization bias and the publisher's own commercial interest in AI services. It is not a CPA-firm benchmark. Read it as the reason value capture has to be measured in your firm rather than assumed from access, and in a seasonal practice the gap is wider still, because an hour returned in July is not the asset an hour returned in March is. The two numbers a partner group most wants do not exist in the public record: the realization impact of AI drafting is unmeasured, and there is no credible mid-market CPA-firm ROI figure. Your own baseline is the only number that will ever be about your firm.
