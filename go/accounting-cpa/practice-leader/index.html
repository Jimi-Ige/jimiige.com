What AI changes

The work moves. The accountability does not.

First drafts are grounded in the practice's own vetted material, so preparers edit upward instead of assembling from zero.

Reviewers can see what was reused and where it came from, which makes review faster and sharper at the same time.

What review corrected is written back to the engagement, so next season's first draft starts from this season's best.

The controls that make it trustworthy
Licensed review before anything is client-facing or file-final, exactly as today. What changes is the quality of what reaches the gate.
AI-assisted drafts labeled as unreviewed until a licensed professional owns them. No unlabeled drafts, ever.
Research support restricted to the firm's approved sources, with every citation verified by a person before anything relies on it.
Conclusions stay human: the tooling assembles, cites, and formats; the licensed professional concludes and signs.
A curated template and prior-year corpus with a named owner and review dates, so drafts build from the practice's best rather than the file share's oldest.

What the published evidence says

Across the 2023 causal studies, AI assistance often narrowed the performance gap between less and more experienced workers on supported tasks, which is a staff-leverage signal for a profession that hires young and teaches through review.

Cross-study inference from Noy & Zhang, 2023; Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond, 2023; Dell'Acqua et al., 2023

Labeled inference from task-level studies, not a measured onboarding return in any CPA firm. It does not mean AI substitutes for professional judgment, licensure, or partner review.
