AI use the firm can explain to any client, in writing, without checking who is asking.

The risk is not that the firm uses AI. It is that the firm cannot say how, where, inside which boundaries, and with whose name on the result. Governed AI operations replaces improvisation with a defensible posture: engagement-level confidentiality boundaries, a stricter default on attest work, licensed accountability at every consequential point, evidence that reconstructs what happened, and a disclosure answer ready before a client, an insurer, or an engagement letter asks for one. AI assists operations and documentation, accountable licensed professionals retain every judgment and every sign-off, and nothing the firm says about its AI use should read as tax, accounting, or audit advice.

When this becomes urgent

The moments that put it on your desk.

Engagement letters and client agreements have started speaking to AI directly, and the firm's posture varies by whoever answers.
A near-miss surfaced: client financial records in an unapproved tool, caught by luck rather than by design.
Nobody has written down who is accountable for AI-assisted content in a workpaper, and reviewers are assuming someone else checked.
An insurer or a client questionnaire asked about AI controls, and the answer was assembled overnight.

What AI changes

The work moves. The accountability does not.

What changes is assembly speed. What never changes is accountability: a named licensed professional answers for every conclusion and every signature.

Every AI-assisted artifact is traceable to its sources and its reviewer, so questions get answers instead of investigations.

The firm's posture becomes one sentence you control, consistent across engagement letters and questionnaires, instead of an improvisation per partner you discover afterward.

The controls that make it trustworthy
Engagement-level confidentiality boundaries checked against each engagement's own terms, because engagement letters are not uniform and the ones that speak to AI are the ones that bind you.
Independence considerations reviewed before any tool arrangement touches an attest engagement, with the strictest posture as the firm's default question.
Licensed review and named sign-off ahead of anything client-facing or file-final, unchanged by how the draft was produced.
Retention rules for prompts and outputs set deliberately against the firm's workpaper retention obligations rather than defaulted.
An incident path with rehearsed client communication, because the plan you need is the one written before the miss.

What the published evidence says

The field experiment that reported gains on tasks inside AI's capability frontier also reported professional quality falling when people relied on it for tasks outside that frontier.

Dell'Acqua et al., 2023 (SSRN)

Consulting tasks at one elite global firm, not accounting work, and where the frontier sits shifts with the model and the domain. It is named here as the risk the review hierarchy exists to catch: in this profession, outside-frontier territory includes whether a technical position has authority behind it and whether the evidence in the file supports the conclusion. That is a control implication for the firm, not advice about any engagement.
