What AI changes

The work moves. The accountability does not.

The sanctioned path becomes the fastest path, the only adoption strategy that has ever beaten shadow IT, and in a CPA firm it has to be fastest in March.

Access follows engagement staffing automatically, so boundaries are enforced by the platform rather than by policy documents.

Every AI-assisted artifact carries its source trail, so questionnaires and internal inspections get answered from the system instead of from archaeology.

The controls that make it trustworthy
Role-based access mirroring engagement staffing, provisioned and revoked with it.
An approved-tool list with a real intake path for new needs, so exceptions have somewhere to go besides the shadows.
Attest engagements held to the strictest posture in the firm by default rather than by exception.
Retention rules for prompts and outputs set against the firm's own workpaper retention obligations, not against a vendor's default.
The platform assists operations and documentation. Judgment and sign-off stay with the licensed professional, by design rather than by configuration.

What the published evidence says

NIST's AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0, January 2023) and its Generative AI Profile (NIST.AI.600-1, July 2024) give a voluntary, vendor-neutral scaffold for governing how AI is designed, deployed, used, and evaluated.

NIST AI RMF 1.0; NIST.AI.600-1

Voluntary frameworks, not mandates and not a professional-liability safe harbor. They have to be adapted to the firm's own confidentiality, independence, and quality obligations, and they say nothing at all about tax, accounting, or audit judgment, which stays with the licensed professional.
