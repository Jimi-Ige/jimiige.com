What AI changes

The work moves. The accountability does not.

Assembly work leaves the critical path: retrieval, drafting, and synthesis run on rails instead of on people.

Fee burn is read against phase completion from timesheets and deliverable status, not against the calendar.

Capacity signals come from assignments and staffing reality, visible while there is still time to act on them.

The controls that make it trustworthy
Role-based access that mirrors project teams, so the tools reach exactly what each team should reach.
QA/QC criteria for AI-assisted content written into the checkpoints where checkers actually work.
Named approval points, so issuing a set has an owner instead of a committee.
Retention and reuse rules set at project close, while the boundaries are still fresh.

What the published evidence says

In a preregistered experiment published in Science, access to a general AI assistant reduced time on professional writing tasks by about 40 percent and raised judged quality by about 18 percent (N of about 453).

Noy & Zhang, Science, 2023

Task-level results on occupation-specific writing, the nearest analog to narratives, reports, and SOQ prose. Not design work, not sealed documents, and not firm-level margin, which is why baselines come before business cases.
