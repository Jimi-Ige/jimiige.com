One governed platform, instead of another tool on the pile.

The design technology estate is already crowded: the CAD and BIM environment, the document management system, the specification tool, a decade of project folders on the file server, and now AI in the studios whether it was approved or not. The question on your desk is whether that becomes a platform you can operate and defend, or a sprawl you get blamed for. Governed AI operations gives you the architecture: project-scoped access that mirrors the project team, curated detail and specification libraries as the only corpus retrieval is allowed to reach, provenance that survives into the record set behind a seal, and a sanctioned path good enough that people stop wanting the shadow one.

AI assists production and documentation. Licensed architects and engineers retain responsible charge, every design decision, and the seal. Nothing in this content is engineering or architectural advice.

When this becomes urgent

The moments that put it on your desk.

Shadow AI is already in the studios, on personal subscriptions and browser tools, and nobody can say which models, drawings, or specifications have passed through them.
Each discipline has procured its own point tool and its own add-ins inside the CAD and BIM environment, every one with a different data posture and none with a shared control model.
Models and documents move to subconsultants and owners through half a dozen channels, so the boundary the firm enforces internally dissolves at the transmittal.
A public owner's security clauses now reach every tool that touches project data, and the estate cannot produce a clean list of which tools those are.

What AI changes

The work moves. The accountability does not.

The sanctioned path runs inside the CAD, specification, and document systems people already work in, which is the only version of adoption that has ever beaten a browser tab.

Access follows project staffing automatically, so client and project boundaries are enforced by the platform, not by policy documents.

Provenance survives the handoff into the record set, so what the checker saw at the QA/QC gate is still reconstructable years later, when a sealed set is the thing being examined.

The controls that make it trustworthy
Role-based access mirroring project teams, provisioned and revoked with staffing, and extended deliberately to subconsultant accounts rather than by exception.
An approved-tool list that covers plugins and add-ins inside the CAD and BIM environment, with a real intake path, because that is where design tools actually enter the estate.
Source traceability and retention rules as platform defaults, not per-studio habits.
Data-exchange rules written into the platform for transmittals to owners and subconsultants, so the boundary holds where the data actually leaves.
An incident path that assumes a miss will eventually happen, naming who tells the project principal, the client, and the insurer.
Proof, not promises

What we would baseline and measure.

No borrowed benchmarks and no invented results. These are the numbers your own pilot would stand on:

Share of AI-assisted project work produced inside the sanctioned path, by studio
Project access reviews passed without remediation, subconsultant accounts included
Turnaround time on owner and public-client security questionnaires
Detail, specification, and project-model repositories under governed retrieval, versus still living on the file server
What the published evidence says

NIST's AI Risk Management Framework (January 2023) and its Generative AI Profile (July 2024) give a voluntary, vendor-neutral scaffold for governing AI design, deployment, use, and evaluation.

NIST AI RMF 1.0; NIST.AI.600-1

Voluntary frameworks, not mandates and not a professional-liability safe harbor. They have to be adapted to a licensed practice's duty of care, its client contracts, and the responsible-charge rules that govern sealed work.
