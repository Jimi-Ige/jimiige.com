Management Consulting · General Counsel / Risk Lead

AI use the firm can defend to two clients who compete with each other.

The risk is not that the firm uses AI. It is that the firm cannot say how, where, and under whose accountability, engagement by engagement, when the client asking is one of two rivals served out of the same practice. Governed AI operations replaces improvisation with a defensible posture: boundaries drawn where the conflicts actually are, a named human answering for every consequential output, evidence that reconstructs what happened, and one disclosure answer ready before the procurement questionnaire arrives.

When this becomes urgent

The moments that put it on your desk.

Master services agreements and statements of work have started carrying AI clauses, and each one was negotiated by a different partner to a different standard.
Two clients in the same practice compete directly, and nothing but staffing habit keeps their material out of one shared retrieval corpus.
A near-miss surfaced: an engagement team put client material into an unapproved tool on deadline, caught by luck rather than by design.
A client's procurement questionnaire arrived mid-pursuit asking how AI touches the engagement, and the answer was assembled overnight by whoever was free.
The workflows that move it

Where the leverage is, for your chair.

1.Knowledge-to-Delivery. Reuse walled by engagement and by conflict, so the firm's memory works for it without carrying one client's context into a rival's deliverable.
2.Proposal-to-Project. Capability reuse with provenance and review, so nothing in a pursuit document traces back to work the firm did for the client's competitor.
3.Client Onboarding & Communication. Client-facing drafting with a named human owner on every send, and a record both sides can rely on when a decision is questioned later.

The full treatment is in the playbook: Grow Delivery Capacity with Governed AI.

What AI changes

The work moves. The accountability does not.

What changes is assembly speed. What never changes is accountability: a named human answers for every consequential output.

Every AI-assisted artifact is traceable to its sources and its reviewer, so a client question gets an answer instead of an investigation.

The firm's posture becomes one sentence you control, given the same way in every pursuit, instead of a hundred improvisations you discover afterward.

The controls that make it trustworthy
AI boundaries set per engagement and checked against that client's MSA or engagement letter, because the clauses now differ from client to client.
Corpus separation between clients who compete, enforced by the platform rather than by whoever remembers the conflict.
Human review and named approval points ahead of every client-facing use, unchanged by how the draft was produced.
An incident path with rehearsed client communication, because the plan you need is the one written before the miss.
One standing answer to the AI section of a procurement questionnaire, reviewed by you, given the same way at every pursuit.
Proof, not promises

What the published evidence says

Quality risk from misapplied AI is not hypothetical: causal field evidence shows professional performance degrades when AI is relied on for tasks outside its capability frontier.

Dell'Acqua et al., 2023 (SSRN)

Frontier boundaries move as models and domains change. The control implication is task routing and accountable review, not prohibition; prohibition just relocates the risk to shadow tools.
