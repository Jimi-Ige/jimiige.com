Grow the firm without betting it on the hiring market.

Your growth plan currently has one engine: hire senior people fast enough to keep up with the work you win. Governed AI adds a second engine. Assembly, drafting, retrieval, and synthesis move onto rails, senior attention returns to winning and standing behind work, and revenue can move faster than headcount without the firm's name paying for it.

When this becomes urgent

The moments that put it on your desk.

The growth plan assumes senior hires the market will not supply at a price the model affords.
Partners are at capacity while the pipeline is still growing, and the choice reads as decline work or dilute quality.
The firm's distinctive expertise is concentrated in a few heads, and succession is a hope rather than a plan.
Clients have started asking how the firm uses AI, and the current answer is individual improvisation.
The workflows that move it

Where the leverage is, for your chair.

1.Proposal-to-Project. Win more of the right work without more partner evenings: qualification stays senior, assembly stops being senior work.
2.Knowledge-to-Delivery. The firm's best thinking compounds instead of retiring with its authors, so every engagement starts above zero.
3.Delivery-to-Executive Insight. Portfolio truth you can run the firm on: status, capacity, and risk traceable to sources, not to Friday memory.

What AI changes

The work moves. The accountability does not.

Research, first drafts, and retrieval come off senior desks, so partner hours concentrate on promise, price, and judgment.

Every pursuit and engagement starts from the firm's best prior work, with provenance, instead of from a blank page.

The operating picture of the firm assembles itself from live artifacts instead of consuming a layer of management time.

The controls that make it trustworthy
Engagement-level boundaries: what one client taught the firm never surfaces in another client's work uninvited.
Human review before anything client-facing, unchanged from today, so accountability never moves.
Provenance on every reused artifact, so the firm always knows what its promises are built from.
A disclosure answer written before clients ask, one a managing partner would happily read aloud.
Proof, not promises

What we would baseline and measure.

No borrowed benchmarks and no invented results. These are the numbers your own pilot would stand on:

Revenue per professional, the honest growth number
Proposal cycle time and win rate, read together
Share of delivery hours on reused versus recreated work
Review turnaround at partner gates
What the published evidence says

Enterprise surveys in 2025 find regular AI use near nine in ten organizations, while only a minority report enterprise-level earnings impact and most experiments never fully scale.

McKinsey State of AI 2025; Deloitte generative-AI survey 2025

Self-reported, large-enterprise-skewed consultancy surveys. The pattern is about value capture, not professional-services ROI; it argues for operating discipline, not for urgency theater.
