Grow the pyramid without betting the firm on the partner market.

Consulting economics run on three dials: the rate you command, the utilization you sustain, and the leverage you run between senior and junior hours. In most firms only the third dial still has room in it, and it is stuck because too much of the pyramid's work needs a partner's hands rather than a partner's judgment. Governed AI moves assembly, retrieval, drafting, and synthesis off senior desks, so the same partnership can carry more delivery without the firm selling work it cannot staff.

When this becomes urgent

The moments that put it on your desk.

The growth plan needs partners the market cannot certify on schedule, because someone clients will actually buy from takes years to make and cannot be hired on a quarter's notice.
The no-bid call keeps arriving after the proposal is half written, so partners are spending their scarcest judgment on pursuits the firm should have declined.
The methods that make a practice distinctive live in a few senior heads, so the practice's value leaves with whoever built it.
Clients have started asking, in pursuit meetings, how the firm uses AI on their work, and the answer changes with whichever partner is in the room.
The workflows that move it

Where the leverage is, for your chair.

1.Proposal-to-Project. The pursuit decision stays a partner call and everything downstream of it stops being one. A faster no-bid is worth more to the pyramid than a faster proposal.
2.Knowledge-to-Delivery. The firm's methods stop living in senior heads, so junior hours start above zero and the leverage ratio can move without the work getting thinner.
3.Delivery-to-Executive Insight. Practice margin, utilization, and bench truth traceable to sources, so the partnership runs the firm on instruments rather than on Friday memory.

The full treatment is in the playbook: Grow Delivery Capacity with Governed AI.

What AI changes

The work moves. The accountability does not.

Assembly comes off senior desks, so partner hours go to the three calls that set the economics: whether to pursue, what to promise, and what to charge.

Junior hours shift from formatting and reconstruction toward the analysis that makes people promotable, which is how leverage moves without dilution.

The operating picture of the firm assembles itself from live artifacts instead of consuming a layer of management time.

The controls that make it trustworthy
Engagement-level boundaries: what one client taught the firm never surfaces in another client's work uninvited.
Human review before anything client-facing, unchanged from today, so accountability never moves.
Provenance on every reused artifact, so the firm always knows what its promises are built from.
A disclosure answer written before clients ask, one a managing partner would happily read aloud.
Proof, not promises

What we would baseline and measure.

No borrowed benchmarks and no invented results. These are the numbers your own pilot would stand on:

Revenue per professional, read against the senior-to-junior leverage ratio that produced it
Senior hours per pursuit, and win rate by pursuit type, read together
Share of delivery hours on reused versus recreated work product
Time from promotion to a new partner carrying their own book, the firm's real capacity clock
What the published evidence says

Enterprise surveys in 2025 find regular AI use near nine in ten organizations, while only a minority report enterprise-level earnings impact and most experiments never fully scale.

McKinsey State of AI 2025; Deloitte generative-AI survey 2025

Self-reported, large-enterprise-skewed consultancy surveys. The pattern is about value capture, not professional-services ROI; it argues for operating discipline, not for urgency theater.
