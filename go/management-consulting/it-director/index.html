One governed platform, instead of thirty ungoverned tools.

The business is already using AI, approved or not. The question on your desk is whether that becomes a platform you can operate and defend, or a sprawl you get blamed for. Governed AI operations gives you the architecture: engagement-scoped access, approved sources, traceability by default, and a sanctioned path good enough that people stop wanting the shadow one.

When this becomes urgent

The moments that put it on your desk.

Shadow AI is already in the building: individual subscriptions, browser tools, and nobody can say what client data has touched them.
Practice groups are procuring point tools independently, each with its own data posture and none with a shared control model.
Client security questionnaires have started asking AI questions the current estate cannot answer cleanly.
Leadership wants AI rolled out and IT is expected to make it safe after the fact.
The workflows that move it

Where the leverage is, for your chair.

1.Knowledge-to-Delivery. The retrieval corpus becomes a governed asset: curated sources, engagement walls, provenance, and an owner.
2.Proposal-to-Project. Capability reuse runs on access controls that mirror engagement staffing, enforced by the platform rather than by memory.
3.Delivery-to-Executive Insight. Reporting synthesis with one source of record per metric, which is an architecture decision before it is a reporting one.

The full treatment is in the playbook: Grow Delivery Capacity with Governed AI.

What AI changes

The work moves. The accountability does not.

The sanctioned path becomes the fastest path, which is the only adoption strategy that has ever beaten shadow IT.

Access follows staffing automatically, so engagement boundaries are enforced by the platform, not by policy documents.

Every AI-assisted artifact carries its source trail, so audits and client questionnaires get answered from the system instead of from archaeology.

The controls that make it trustworthy
Role-based access mirroring engagement staffing, provisioned and revoked with it.
An approved-tool list with a real intake path for new needs, so exceptions have somewhere to go besides the shadows.
Source traceability and retention rules as platform defaults, not per-team habits.
An incident path that assumes a miss will eventually happen and names who does what when it does.
Proof, not promises

What the published evidence says

NIST's AI Risk Management Framework (January 2023) and its Generative AI Profile (July 2024) give a voluntary, vendor-neutral scaffold for governing AI design, deployment, use, and evaluation.

NIST AI RMF 1.0; NIST.AI.600-1

Voluntary frameworks, not mandates and not a professional-liability safe harbor. They have to be adapted to the firm's own duty-of-care and client obligations.
