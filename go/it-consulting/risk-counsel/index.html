IT Consulting · Security & Risk Lead

An AI posture you could walk a client's security team through.

The risk is not that the firm uses AI. It is that the firm cannot say where, under whose account, inside which client environment, and under what controls. Your exposure is unusual: the firm works inside other organizations' systems, holds their credentials, and may serve their competitors. Governed AI operations replaces improvisation with a defensible posture: client and contract boundaries, credentials out of prompts, provenance on generated code, an accountable engineer at every gate, and evidence that reconstructs what happened.

When this becomes urgent

The moments that put it on your desk.

Client master agreements and security addenda have started speaking to AI directly, and they do not all say the same thing.
A near-miss surfaced: client material or a credential in an unapproved tool, caught by luck rather than by design.
Generated code has shipped into a client's production environment with no provenance record and no license posture behind it.
A client's security team, an insurer, or an acquirer asked about AI controls, and the answer was assembled overnight.
The workflows that move it

Where the leverage is, for your chair.

1.Client Environment Onboarding. Access, credential handling, and each client's AI rules settled before the first assisted commit, not after the first incident.
2.Build on What You Built. Reuse inside client and contract walls with IP status recorded where the reuse happens, so the firm's memory never becomes another client's property leaving the building.
3.Architecture & Code Review Gates. A named senior engineer accountable for what merges, whoever or whatever produced it, with AI-assisted work marked at the gate.

The full treatment is in the playbook: Deliver More Projects with Governed AI.

What AI changes

The work moves. The accountability does not.

What changes is assembly speed. What never changes is accountability: a named engineer answers for every consequential artifact.

Every generated or reused component is traceable to its source and its reviewer, so questions get answers instead of investigations.

The firm's posture becomes one sentence you control, instead of a hundred improvisations you discover.

The controls that make it trustworthy
Per-project AI posture checked against that client's contract and security addendum, because they differ and some now speak to AI directly.
Credentials and client secrets never in prompts or tool context, enforced technically rather than by policy.
A provenance record for generated code written as the code is written, because it cannot be reconstructed later.
Senior engineering review and a named approval before anything merges or reaches a client environment.
An incident path with rehearsed client communication, because the plan you need is the one written before the miss.

What the published evidence says

NIST's AI Risk Management Framework (January 2023) and its Generative AI Profile (July 2024) give a voluntary, vendor-neutral scaffold for governing AI design, deployment, use, and evaluation.

NIST AI RMF 1.0; NIST.AI.600-1

Voluntary frameworks, not mandates and not a liability safe harbor. They have to be adapted to the firm's own client contracts and to the security obligations that come with working inside someone else's environment.
