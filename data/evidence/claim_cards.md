# Claim Cards — Governed AI Ops for Professional Services

Publication-ready cards for the highest-utility claims. Each card includes classification, sources, audience, use, and mandatory caveat language.

**Classification key**
- **Fact:** Directly supported by cited primary source as stated
- **Inference:** Reasonable extension from multiple sources; not independently measured
- **Strategic judgment:** Product/playbook design choice requiring practitioner calibration

---

## Card C01 — Task-level writing productivity (causal)

**Publication-ready claim:**  
In a preregistered experiment published in *Science* (2023), access to ChatGPT reduced average time on mid-level professional writing tasks by about 40% and raised output quality by about 18% among college-educated professionals (N≈453).

**Classification:** Fact (causal RCT)

**Direct supporting sources:**  
- Noy, S. & Zhang, W. “Experimental evidence on the productivity effects of generative artificial intelligence.” *Science* (2023). https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adh2586  
- Working paper detail (MIT): https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/inline-files/Noy_Zhang_1.pdf  

**Audience/persona:** Practice Leader; COO; Managing Partner/CEO  

**Industries / workflows:** Cross-industry knowledge work · Knowledge-to-Delivery (drafting) · Proposal-to-Project (draft sections)  

**Recommended content use:** Playbook productivity chapter; assessment evidence callout; LinkedIn with full caveat  

**Caveat language:**  
“Results come from controlled, occupation-specific writing tasks—not full client engagements, regulated advice, or confidential matter workflows. Quality was evaluator-scored on experimental outputs. Do not treat as firm-level margin or realization improvement.”

**Ledger IDs:** U-AI-001, U-AI-002

## Card C02 — Consulting field experiment (causal)

**Publication-ready claim:**  
In a preregistered field experiment with 758 consultants at Boston Consulting Group, GPT-4 access on tasks inside the model’s capability frontier increased tasks completed by 12.2%, speed by 25.1%, and quality by more than 40% versus a no-AI control group (Dell’Acqua et al., 2023).

**Classification:** Fact (causal field RCT)

**Direct supporting sources:**  
- Dell’Acqua et al., “Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier,” SSRN (Sept 2023). https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4573321  

**Audience/persona:** Practice Leader; COO; Managing Partner (management consulting primary)

**Industries / workflows:** Management consulting (primary) · Knowledge-to-Delivery · research, drafting, analysis  

**Recommended content use:** Management consulting pack anchor; universal playbook with elite-firm label  

**Caveat language:**  
“Evidence is from one elite global firm and experimental tasks. It does not automatically generalize to mid-market firms (20–200 employees), multi-client confidentiality environments, or all task types. Pair always with the jagged-frontier quality risk (Card C03).”

**Ledger IDs:** U-AI-003, CONS-001

## Card C03 — Jagged technological frontier (causal)

**Publication-ready claim:**  
The same BCG field experiment shows a “jagged technological frontier”: AI can sharply improve performance on some knowledge tasks while harming quality when applied to tasks outside current AI capabilities if professionals rely on it inappropriately.

**Classification:** Fact (causal field RCT)

**Direct supporting sources:**  
- Dell’Acqua et al. (2023). https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4573321  

**Audience/persona:** Risk/GC; Practice Leader; COO; CIO  

**Industries / workflows:** All knowledge PS · Quality review · human accountability · task routing  

**Recommended content use:** Governance and quality chapters; assessment items on review gates  

**Caveat language:**  
“Which tasks sit inside or outside the frontier depends on the model, prompt pattern, and domain, and shifts over time. Human accountable review remains a professional requirement independent of average productivity gains.”

**Ledger ID:** U-AI-004

## Card C04 — Novice leverage in support work (causal / quasi-experimental)

**Publication-ready claim:**  
In a staggered deployment study of a generative AI assistant across 5,179 customer-support agents, productivity (issues resolved per hour) rose about 14% on average, including about 34% for novice and lower-skilled workers, with minimal gains for the most skilled (Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond, NBER w31161).

**Classification:** Fact in origin setting; transfer to professional advisory work is Inference  

**Direct supporting sources:**  
- https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w31161/w31161.pdf  
- NBER Digest summary: https://www.nber.org/digest/20236/measuring-productivity-impact-generative-ai  

**Audience/persona:** COO; Practice Leader; CIO (MSP/IT hypothesis)

**Industries / workflows:** Customer support origin · MSP/IT service desk (hypothesis) · onboarding · knowledge encoding  

**Recommended content use:** Knowledge-reuse thesis; MSP pack with transfer label  

**Caveat language:**  
“Setting is software customer-support chat (many agents outside the U.S.), not legal, audit, or consulting advice. Agents could ignore suggestions. Do not cite as proof of professional-services billable-margin improvement.”

**Ledger IDs:** U-AI-005, IT-001

## Card C08 — Predicted hours are not measured ROI

**Classification:** Fact that the prediction was reported; **not** a fact that savings occurred  

**Direct supporting sources:**  
- https://www.thomsonreuters.com/en-us/posts/innovation/future-of-professionals-report-ai-set-to-save-professionals-12-hours-per-week/  
- Report PDF: https://www.thomsonreuters.com/content/dam/ewp-m/documents/thomsonreuters/en/pdf/reports/future-of-professionals-report-2024.pdf  

**Audience/persona:** CFO; Managing Partner (expectation-setting)

**Industries / workflows:** Business-case hygiene · measurement  

**Recommended content use:** Assessment item contrasting expectation vs evidence; never hero metric without label  

**Caveat language:**  
“Vendor-sponsored global survey of predicted time savings. Not a time-motion study. Hours predicted may not convert to utilization, realization, or profit.”

**Ledger IDs:** U-ADOPT-005, U-ADOPT-006
